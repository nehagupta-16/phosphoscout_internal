Overview

What the variant is and why it's being analyzed

Mutation (CDS)
Mutation (AA)p.A686T

OGT p.A686T is a missense change within the catalytic domain of O-GlcNAc transferase, an essential enzyme mediating widespread O-GlcNAcylation and implicated in OGT-CDG/XLID when mutated [1, 2, 3]. We did not find mutation-specific clinical reports for p.A686T, but residue 686 maps within the human OGT GT-B catalytic core resolved structurally [1]. In our analysis of the local 29-mer, the A→T substitution creates a central threonine phospho-acceptor within an evidence-supported window that is solvent accessible (central ±3), and kinase scoring indicates substantial serine/threonine-kinase specificity increases for BMPR1A/B in this window (computational assessment).

Hypotheses

Mechanistic explanations generated from the overview and literature.

OGT A686T creates a BMPR1A/B-rewired phospho-switch at T686 that allosterically reduces OGT catalytic efficiency, contributing to OGT-CDG/XLID via BMP–SMAD neurodevelopmental signaling sensitivity

The OGT A686T substitution creates a new solvent-accessible phospho-acceptor at T686 (center_pos 686) within the catalytic GT-B core [1]. Kinase–substrate specificity modeling predicts a gain of BMPR1A and BMPR1B recognition at this new T686 site (gain events), implying that canonical BMP receptor kinases could preferentially phosphorylate OGT at/near T686 under BMP pathway activity (a prediction requiring direct phospho-T686 measurement) [mutation_impact_payload]. Because residue 686 maps within the catalytic domain architecture that supports OGT activity, phosphorylation at T686 is hypothesized to allosterically reduce OGT catalytic efficiency and globally perturb O-GlcNAcylation homeostasis (directionality to be tested biochemically) [1, 3]. In neurodevelopmental settings where BMP–SMAD1/5/8 signaling is active, this creates a context-dependent “BMP-on → OGT-off” coupling that could exacerbate OGT-CDG/XLID phenotypes by further destabilizing O-GlcNAc cycling (prediction) [2, 4, 5].

Disease context

  • OGT-CDG / X-linked intellectual disability (XLID) [2, 5]
  • Neurodevelopmental learning phenotypes from disrupted O-GlcNAc cycling (model-organism support) [4]

Predicted observations

  • A phospho-signal at OGT T686 is detectable specifically (or strongly enriched) in the A686T mutant compared with WT, measured by targeted MS (PRM/SRM) on the 679–693 region or a custom phospho-T686 antibody (direction and stoichiometry to be quantified) [mutation_impact_payload].
  • BMP pathway stimulation (e.g., BMP4/BMP7) increases the probability/occupancy of pT686 in A686T cells, and BMPR1A/B inhibition reduces that signal, consistent with predicted BMPR1A/B gain of recognition at T686 (discriminates from generic stress-kinase phosphorylation) [mutation_impact_payload, [6].
  • Compared with WT OGT, A686T shows reduced catalytic activity in vitro when pre-phosphorylated at T686 (or after incubation with active BMPR1A/B kinase domains), with corresponding decreases in global protein O-GlcNAc levels in cells under BMP stimulation (directionality to be directly measured) [1, 3], mutation_impact_payload].
  • Neuronal differentiation or synaptic/habituation phenotypes are more sensitive to BMP stimulation in A686T backgrounds, and this sensitivity is reduced by a T686A (phospho-null) rescue in the A686T protein (mechanism-discriminating epistasis tying the phenotype to the new phosphosite) [4], mutation_impact_payload].
Laboratory validation

Suggested models

  • HEK293 or neuronal progenitor-like cells engineered to express OGT WT vs A686T at near-endogenous levels (e.g., CRISPR knock-in or rescue in OGT-depleted background) [2].
  • iPSC-derived neurons (patient-relevant neurodevelopmental context) with isogenic OGT A686T vs WT [5].

Assays

  • Targeted phosphoproteomics (PRM/SRM) for the OGT 679–693 peptide to quantify pT686 and non-phospho peptide across conditions (baseline vs BMP ligand; ± BMPR1A/B inhibitor) [mutation_impact_payload, [6].
  • Kinase perturbation: pharmacologic BMP type I receptor inhibition (e.g., ALK2/3/6 inhibitors) and/or BMPR1A/B knockdown/CRISPRi to test dependency of the pT686 signal and downstream effects [6].
  • Site perturbation epistasis: express OGT A686T+T686A (phospho-null) and OGT A686T+T686E (phosphomimetic proxy) in an OGT-replacement system to test causal contribution of the new site to OGT activity and cellular phenotypes [1, 3].
  • OGT function readouts: in vitro OGT enzymatic assays (UDP-GlcNAc donor + peptide/protein acceptor), plus cell-level global O-GlcNAc immunoblotting (RL2/CTD110.6) and substrate-specific O-GlcNAc proteomics to quantify directionality [3].
  • Pathway activity controls: measure pSMAD1/5/8 as a BMP pathway activation marker to ensure BMP perturbations are on-target and to separate BMP signaling effects from OGT-specific effects [6].

Controls and confounders

  • Control for expression level/localization of WT vs A686T OGT, since altered stability or mislocalization could mimic catalytic changes [3].
  • Include catalytically impaired OGT control (known active-site mutant) to benchmark the magnitude of any activity loss attributable to T686 phosphorylation vs generic loss-of-function [1].
  • Use orthogonal BMP pathway perturbations (ligand vs inhibitor vs receptor knockdown) to avoid attributing off-target inhibitor effects to BMPR1A/B dependence [6].

Feasibility notes

The core discriminator is epistasis: if BMP stimulation changes phenotype/activity only when the T686 phosphosite is present (A686T) and is abolished by T686A in the A686T background, this supports a causal phospho-switch mechanism rather than generic catalytic destabilization from the missense change alone [mutation_impact_payload, [3].

Limitations
  • BMPR1A/B are typically membrane receptor kinases; whether they (or downstream kinases in BMP signaling) directly phosphorylate cytosolic/nuclear OGT at T686 is uncertain and must be tested with kinase mapping and compartment-specific experiments [6].
  • The kinase-event data indicate predicted specificity rewiring, not actual phosphorylation; pT686 existence and BMP-dependence require direct phospho mapping and quantification [mutation_impact_payload].
  • Even if pT686 occurs, the direction of its effect on OGT catalytic activity (inhibitory vs activating) is unknown a priori and must be established using biochemical assays [1, 3].

Context

brain glutamatergic neurons, Alzheimer disease vs normal

Mechanistic expectations
  • Hypothesis proposes a BMP–SMAD-dependent, post-translational phospho-switch on OGT A686T (gain of BMPR1A/B phosphorylation at T686) that reduces OGT catalytic efficiency (“BMP-on → OGT-off”), especially in neurodevelopmental settings
  • No explicit prediction for OGT, BMPR1A, or BMPR1B mRNA direction; mechanism is PTM/activity-centric
Observed results
  • OGT up in AD neurons (logFC +0.239, d 0.361, q 0.012)
  • BMPR1A slightly down (logFC −0.064, d −0.085, q 0.020)
  • BMPR1B ~no change (logFC +0.007, d 0.014, q 0.091)
Alignment assessment
  • Strong OGT mRNA increase does not by itself indicate higher catalytic activity; PTM effects can decouple from mRNA levels
  • Slight BMPR1A decrease and minimal BMPR1B change in neurons do not establish enhanced BMP receptor signaling at the mRNA level; kinase activity cannot be inferred from receptor transcripts alone
  • Given the PTM-centric mechanism and adult AD context (not developmental XLID/OGT-CDG), these mRNA shifts neither confirm nor refute the proposed phospho-switch mechanism
