Overview

What the variant is and why it's being analyzed

FieldValue
GeneOGT
Mutation (CDS)
Mutation (AA)p.N648Y

OGT p.N648Y (ASN648TYR) lies in the catalytic domain and has been reported in X-linked intellectual disability/OGT-CDG cohorts with reduced catalytic activity and structural perturbation of the catalytic site [4, 5]. OGT mediates nutrient- and stress-responsive O-GlcNAcylation of many proteins, with broad roles in gene regulation and cell signaling [2]. In our 29-mer analysis centered on residue 648, the variant creates a novel central tyrosine phospho-acceptor at position 648 within a solvent-accessible region (RSA ≥ 0.1 within ±3). Kinase specificity scoring on the gained Y-centered motif indicates a substantial mutant gain for the tyrosine kinase TNK1 (WT 0.0 → Mut 96.69 percentile).

Hypotheses

Mechanistic explanations generated from the overview and literature.

OGT p.N648Y creates a TNK1-targetable Y648 motif that triggers inhibitory phosphorylation near the OGT catalytic core, further suppressing O-GlcNAcylation and contributing to XLID/OGT-CDG phenotypes

OGT p.N648Y (catalytic domain) creates a new solvent-accessible tyrosine phospho-acceptor at center_pos 648 (…GARYEL… vs …GARNEL…) that is predicted to gain TNK1 kinase recognition (TNK1: 0.0→96.69 percentile; direction=gain), representing specificity rewiring rather than an observed phosphorylation change [4]. If TNK1 phosphorylates OGT at Y648 in cells (to be tested by site-specific phospho readouts), this would plausibly allosterically perturb the catalytic region and/or interfere with substrate/UDP-GlcNAc handling, further reducing OGT activity beyond the catalytic impairment already reported for N648Y, lowering global O-GlcNAc and exacerbating neurodevelopmental dysfunction seen in XLID/OGT-CDG [4, 5]. Because O-GlcNAcylation broadly cross-talks with phosphorylation to regulate transcription and stress-response programs, an additional phosphorylation-dependent downshift in OGT function would be expected to amplify downstream signaling/transcriptional defects relevant to neurodevelopmental phenotypes [2, 3].

Disease context

  • Neurodevelopmental defects in OGT-CDG mouse models [6]

Predicted observations

  • Phospho readout: detection of phosphorylation at OGT Y648 specifically in the p.N648Y background (but not WT) by targeted LC–MS/MS phosphoproteomics or a custom pY648-OGT antibody; signal should be sensitive to TNK1 perturbation (directionality to be empirically established) [4].
  • Kinase-dependence discriminator: TNK1 inhibition/knockdown should reduce the Y648 phospho signal and partially restore global O-GlcNAc levels (e.g., RL2/CTD110.6 immunoblot) in N648Y-expressing cells if Y648 phosphorylation is inhibitory; the opposite pattern would support an activating model, directly falsifying the inhibitory-phospho hypothesis [2].
  • Site-causality discriminator: an epistasis test where OGT N648Y/Y648F (phospho-dead) shows higher OGT catalytic output (higher global O-GlcNAc and/or higher O-GlcNAc on sentinel substrates) than OGT N648Y alone, despite having the same catalytic-domain missense background, indicating an added PTM-dependent suppression layer [2, 4].
Laboratory validation

Suggested models

  • Patient-derived fibroblasts or lymphoblastoid cells carrying OGT p.N648Y (if available) [4].
  • Neuronal models: iPSC-derived cortical neurons expressing OGT WT vs N648Y vs N648Y/Y648F (genome-edited) to align with neurodevelopmental phenotypes [6].
  • HEK293 or neuroblastoma cells for biochemical mechanism dissection with controlled TNK1 expression/activity (transient transfection) [2].

Assays

  • Targeted phosphoproteomics for OGT pY648 (±TNK1 inhibition/knockdown) to validate the predicted TNK1-dependent phosphorylation event [4].
  • In vitro kinase assay: recombinant TNK1 + recombinant OGT catalytic domain fragments (WT vs N648Y) followed by MS mapping to test whether TNK1 can directly phosphorylate Y648 (and to quantify kinetics) [4].
  • OGT activity readouts: global O-GlcNAc immunoblot and/or quantitative O-GlcNAc proteomics; optionally measure OGA/OGT substrate-specific O-GlcNAc changes as functional outputs [2, 3].
  • Phenotype assays in neuronal models: neurite outgrowth, synapse marker quantification, and neuronal maturation/readouts used in OGT-ID models to connect biochemical changes to neurodevelopmental phenotypes [6].

Controls and confounders

  • Control for baseline catalytic deficit of N648Y independent of phosphorylation by comparing N648Y vs N648Y/Y648F under identical expression and UDP-GlcNAc availability [4].
  • Confirm TNK1 specificity by including a kinase-dead TNK1 control and/or parallel perturbation of related ACK family kinase TNK2/ACK1 to rule out off-target effects (interpretation limited to dependence rather than exclusivity) [2].
  • Account for cellular stress/nutrient conditions that alter O-GlcNAc globally; perform assays under matched glucose/GlcNAc supplementation and stress conditions [1, 2].

Feasibility notes

Because the key event is a newly created phosphotyrosine site, MS-based detection (targeted PRM/SRM) is likely the fastest route to validate Y648 phosphorylation and its TNK1 dependence before investing in custom phospho-antibodies [4].

Limitations
  • Only one significant kinase-event (TNK1 gain) is provided, so the kinase-rewiring evidence is strong in magnitude but narrow in breadth, increasing risk of overfitting to a single predicted edge [4].
  • Whether Y648 phosphorylation is inhibitory vs activating for OGT catalysis is unknown a priori and must be established experimentally via phospho-readouts plus functional O-GlcNAc measurements (directionality is a prediction, not a fact) [2].
  • TNK1 expression/activity in relevant developing neuronal cell types is not established in the provided sources, so cell-type relevance must be empirically confirmed (e.g., by proteomics or activity assays) [6].
